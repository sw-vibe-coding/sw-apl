Every example below was run by sw-apl and its result recorded under it; in the sw-apl in your browser, )LOAD 1 BIRDS in the (B) tab gives the same answers.

Birds and combinators

A combinator is a function of functions. The Bluebird takes two functions and composes them; the Mockingbird takes a function and applies it to itself. Raymond Smullyan's To Mock a Mockingbird (1985) made each combinator a bird, and the names have stuck.

APL is a language of functions, but in the APL of 1970 a function could not be handed to another: an argument is always an array. The (A) '70 BIRDS gets as far as that allows, over a table of primitives. (B) '75 has what makes the rest possible: execute, ⍎, which runs a character vector as a line of APL. 

⍎'2+3'

5

So in (B) a bird is given a function by its name – its glyph, or the name of a defined function – as characters, and calls it by building a line around the name and executing it. Nothing else is needed, and no table stands between the bird and the function: any function will do, primitive or defined.

The Thrush: calling a function by name

The Thrush is the pattern every bird follows, so it comes first. X T F applies F to X: it catenates the name and ' X' and executes the line. 

∇R←X T F
R←⍎F,' X'
∇

The line runs inside T, where X is T's own argument, so the function named applies to the value T was given. A glyph is a name: 

5 T '⍳'

1 2 3 4 5

and so is a defined function's name. FACT is the usual recursive factorial, which the workspace keeps as a function to give to birds: 

∇R←FACT N
→(N>1)/MORE
R←1
→0
MORE:R←N×FACT N-1
∇

6 T 'FACT'

720

Birds on values: the Idiot and the Kestrel

Two birds apply nothing, so they need no execute. The Idiot gives back its argument, and the Kestrel keeps its left argument whatever its right one is. 

∇R←I X
R←X
∇

∇R←X K Y
R←X
∇

'KEEP' K 1 2 3

The Kite: a bird made of two others

Smullyan makes the Kite from the Kestrel and the Idiot: it is K I, the Kestrel given the Idiot, and it keeps its right argument. In (B) that construction can be written as he wrote it. 'I' K X is the name 'I', whatever X is, and that name applied to Y is Y: 

∇R←X KI Y
⍝ K GIVEN THE IDIOT: 'I' K X IS 'I', AND THAT NAME, APPLIED TO Y,
⍝ IS Y.
R←⍎('I' K X),' Y'
∇

7 KI 9

9

Two functions at once: the Bluebird and the Starling

The Bluebird composes: FG B X is F G X. An APL function has at most two arguments, and one of them is X, so both functions come in one character vector, with a space between them since a name can be longer than one character. The line ⍎ runs is then F G X itself: 

∇R←FG B X
⍝ FG IS 'F G', TWO FUNCTIONS BY NAME OR GLYPH: F G X.
R←⍎FG,' X'
∇

'⌽ ⍳' B 5
'FACT ⌈' B 3.2

      '⌽ ⍳' B 5
5 4 3 2 1
      'FACT ⌈' B 3.2
24

The Starling is X F G X: F between X and G X. With '+ ⌽', each element is added to its mirror image: 

∇R←FG S X
⍝ FG IS 'F G': X F G X.
R←⍎'X ',FG,' X'
∇

'+ ⌽' S ⍳5

6 6 6 6 6

One function, two ways round: the Cardinal and the Warbler

The Cardinal flips the arguments of a dyadic function: F C Y Z is Z F Y. It takes the two values as a pair, since F has the left argument's place: 

∇R←F C YZ
R←⍎'YZ[2] ',F,' YZ[1]'
∇

'-' C 10 3

¯7

The Warbler gives a dyadic function the same argument on both sides: F W X is X F X. HYP, the hypotenuse, is here to be given to it, and × does as well: 

∇R←F W X
R←⍎'X ',F,' X'
∇

∇R←A HYP B
R←((A*2)+B*2)*0.5
∇

'HYP' W 3
'×' W 5

      'HYP' W 3
4.2426
      '×' W 5
25

The Mockingbird: a function applied to itself

The Mockingbird is λx.x x. A function is given here by its name, so applied to itself means applied to its own name. SHOUT puts an exclamation mark after its argument, and M 'SHOUT' is SHOUT given 'SHOUT': 

∇R←M F
⍝ F APPLIED TO ITSELF: TO ITS OWN NAME.
R←⍎F,' F'
∇

∇R←SHOUT X
R←X,'!'
∇

M 'SHOUT'

SHOUT!

The Sage: recursion without a name

The Sage, Y, takes a function and gives back its fixed point, which is recursion without the function ever naming itself. FSTEP is one step of a factorial that calls itself only through its left argument, SELF, a line of characters that says how: 

∇R←SELF FSTEP N
⍝ ONE STEP OF THE FACTORIAL. IT NEVER NAMES ITSELF: SELF IS HOW.
→(N>1)/MORE
R←1
→0
MORE:R←N×⍎SELF,' N-1'
∇

The Sage supplies SELF: a quoted copy of the step's name followed by Y, so that executing SELF with an argument goes round the Sage again. 

∇R←F Y X;SELF
⍝ THE FIXED POINT OF F. F IS DYADIC: ITS LEFT ARGUMENT IS HOW TO
⍝ CALL ITSELF, AS CHARACTERS, SO IT RECURSES WITHOUT ITS OWN NAME.
SELF←'''',F,''' Y'
R←⍎'SELF ',F,' X'
∇

'FSTEP' Y 6
FACT 6

      'FSTEP' Y 6
720
      FACT 6
720

APL evaluates an argument before the function that takes it, so a Sage built by self-application alone would never stop building itself. The strict fixed point, Z, delays the self-application by a step, and that is what Y is here: the line in SELF is only characters until FSTEP decides to run it.

What is still missing

With execute, every bird that APL of 1970 could not write is here. NOTHERE says what (B) still lacks: a function as a value. 

∇NOTHERE
'WITH EXECUTE, EVERY BIRD THAT APL\360 COULD NOT WRITE IS HERE:'

'MISSING IS A FUNCTION AS A VALUE.'
''

'CANNOT GIVE BACK A FUNCTION: B RETURNS F G X, NOT THE NEW'

'PRIMITIVES ONLY, IN THIS APL AS IN APL\360.'

The workspace file

A saved workspace is APL that could have been typed: loading it runs it. It opens with lines that say what it is – its modes, where it came from, when it was saved, the settings it keeps – and names itself; then come the functions, in the order )SAVE writes them, which is by name. 

⍝ sw-apl workspace. Re-executable APL: loading it runs it.
⍝!MODES (B)
⍝!SOURCE sw-apl -- written for this repository. MIT, (c) 2026 Michael A Wright.
⍝!SAVED 20.41.27 09/22/26
⍝!LINK 16807
⍝!ORIGIN 1
⍝!DIGITS 5
⍝!WIDTH 64
)WSID BIRDS

That order is the file's, not the reader's. The block below puts the pieces of this document together in it, and tangling the document writes the workspace: 

<<workspace-header>>
<<fn-b>>
<<fn-c>>
<<fn-describe>>
<<fn-fact>>
